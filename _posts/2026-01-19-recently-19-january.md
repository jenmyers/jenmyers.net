### Reading/Watching/Listening

- Since I was a young person reading too much Kerouac (I'll admit there was too much, although I'm also frustrated with the modern posturing that flattens him into an unfashionable caricature of outdated sins and maybe someday I'll have more to say about that), I have intended to dive into the writing of Gary Synder, the model for the primary figure of [_The Dharma Bums_](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/f4f9e763-5f15-4489-8f30-cef084e0a029). Some thirty years later, I finally got around to it by reading his essay collection, [_The Practice of the Wild_](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/47e3198f-73e6-47b0-83a9-8451d3569e4f), and I should have been reading more from him all along. A tremendous work.

- While I usually am a strict "book before movie" type of gal, sometimes I won't stumble across the novels from which iconic films were adapted until long after the fact, which was the case with Rumer Godden's [_Black Narcissus_](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/616d091f-c92e-451c-b4df-fdf0bc5aa667). But the book is as vivid and arresting as the film is.

- [_The El_](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/f14af6f3-bca5-44c8-8fbd-a01183781f63), by Theodore C. Van Alst Jr. What a howling piece of Chicago poetic prose.

- [_The Desert_](https://letterboxd.com/film/a-desert/) is a deliriously strange and disorienting deconstruction of a horror-noir film. So I dug it a lot.

- [_Strange Harvest_](https://letterboxd.com/film/strange-harvest-2024/) is an oddly compelling mock documentary mashup of true crime and cosmic horror that, in my opinion, could have gone further into the latter but still turns out a pretty solid film at a unique genre intersection.
