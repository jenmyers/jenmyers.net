### Reading/Watching/Listening

- I finally got a hold of the long out-of-print [_Between Two Fires_](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/18140459-40bf-4293-ac04-e5564ba8e108) by Christopher Buehlman and, yeah, turns out it's a banger, just like everyone says. Glad it's more easily accessible to folks again.

- I read William Peter Blatty's [_The Exorcist_](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/59fb63cf-dca1-443d-8903-4a33466d4d27), which also, believe it or not, is pretty good. It holds up next to its film adaptation—although its sequel, [_Legion_](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/1e761a7f-bb45-4ce2-a103-97019ef3cc38), pales in comparison to its film adaptation, [_The Exorcist III_](https://letterboxd.com/film/the-exorcist-iii/), which was also written and directed by Blatty.

- This past summer, I went to the [Gertrude Abercrombie retrospective at the Milwaukee Art Musuem](https://mam.org/exhibitions/whole-world/) (now departed but I highly recommend looking up her work), and I was taken by her repeated depictions of herself as the Countess Nerona, a fictional character from Wilkie Collins's [_The Haunted Hotel_](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/b767a838-d1e2-4bba-ac54-5d84c3278d28). So I read _The Haunted Hotel_, and it is, to my mind, something of a Victorian Gothic masterwork. Who wouldn't want to go romantically, lavishly, murderously mad in old Venice? Maybe just me and Gertrude.

- I'm currently reading Dan Egan's [_The Death and Life of the Great Lakes_](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/1626c066-e5c4-495c-9ae6-1fa70c97c3eb), which is painting a much more dire picture of the need to attend to the health of my beloved Great Lakes than I was previously acquainted with and it's slightly stressing me out but also very important.

- You better believe I saw Ken Russell's newly restored [_The Devils_](https://letterboxd.com/film/the-devils/) in the theater and I hope you did too (I think there's still time, run, run, run.)

- While quite a different vibe, [_Coyote vs. Acme_](https://letterboxd.com/film/coyote-vs-acme/) was also excellent and deserves a watch.

- In this household, we appreciate Kiyoshi Kurosawa in all his moods, including the pensive historical murder mystery turned character study that is [_The Samurai and the Prisoner_](https://letterboxd.com/film/the-samurai-and-the-prisoner/).

- My deep appreciation for director Mark Jenkin that begun with his previous feature [_Enys Men_](https://letterboxd.com/film/enys-men/) (one of my favorites in recent years) was firmly cemented with [_Rose of Nevada_](https://letterboxd.com/film/rose-of-nevada/), which I was lucky enough to see with him in attendance. Awed and inspired by his unconventional cinematography and sound design.

- Quick hits on the women-focused film front: [_Saccharine_](https://letterboxd.com/film/saccharine-2026/), the latest from Natalie Erika James, goes places; [_Mārama_](https://letterboxd.com/film/marama/), a flawed but powerful and gorgeous gothic rebuke of colonialism from a Māori perspective; and [_Soulm8te_](https://letterboxd.com/film/soulm8te/), from director Kate Dolan, which might be too slight to capture much attention, but I thought it had something worth saying.

- Go watch [_The Cycle_](https://letterboxd.com/film/the-cycle-2026-2/). Don't question it. Don't research it. Just watch and have fun.

- You all saw Brandi Carlile's "Uninvited" cover, right? Just checking.
