### Links

- "Shelves of unexplored ideas propel us to continue reading, continue learning, and never be comfortable that we know enough. They provide the foundation for a healthy and robust intellectual humility." Here's the philosophical justification for all your unread book piles that you've been waiting for: [What our shelves of unread books teach us about ourselves](https://bigthink.com/neuropsych/do-i-own-too-many-books/).

- [Maintaining the "Disneyland of the dead," London's Highgate Cemetery.](https://longreads.com/2025/07/22/problem-of-burying-dead-bodies/)

- [Abigail Goldman's tiny crime scene dioramas](https://www.abigailgoldman.com/documentary) (which she calls, appropriately, "Dieoramas," which I assume you have to say in a Crypt Keeper voice every time you say it).

- I recently learned about the artist [Anne Adams and the neurological condition that led to her overwhelming obsession with Maurice Ravel's _Boléro_](https://www.newscientist.com/article/dn13599-bolero-beautiful-symptom-of-a-terrible-disease/). It is sad but fascinating.

- The five-hour cut of Joe Dante's legendary and difficult-to-see video clip collage, [_The Movie Orgy_](https://archive.org/details/the-movie-orgy-1968), is available on the Internet Archive. If that's your idea of a good time.

- "More generally, engineered wonder is when you take something that sparks a genuine flare of interest, and you pursue it to a degree that’s remarkable (or, depending on who you ask, perhaps even absurd)." [On engineered wonder.](https://calnewport.com/on-engineered-wonder/)

### Reading/Watching/Listening

- I _loved_ Kat Dunn's [_Hungerstone_](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/40fa01e4-4914-46dc-a51c-de059690f4dc). Nothing more to say, run don't walk, etc.

- [_The Night Guest_](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/da72a411-1e4f-4db7-ae06-b189cd869f5c) by Hildur Knútsdóttir is worth the slow burn (in fact, I could have used an even slower burn that dug even deeper into the character's past, but a fan nonetheless).

- I recently rewatched a little-known favorite film of mine, [_Cracks_](https://letterboxd.com/film/cracks/), for an upcoming podcast episode, and much of the research around it referenced Muriel Spark's novel, [_The Prime of Miss Jean Brodie_](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/1c9003a8-8be9-4beb-b22a-e3b89af55097). So I read that. The resemblance to _Cracks_ is superficial, but I liked it quite a bit for what it is: a wry and slightly melancholy character study of a woman lost to her own projections and her ultimately unsavory influence on the young girls around her.

- I'm late getting to [_The Monkey_](https://letterboxd.com/film/the-monkey-2025/) because I suspected I wouldn't like it very much. Turns out I was wrong. A darkly comic exaggeration of the apparent absurdity of death and finding a way to live in the face of it. Those who think it lacks substance might want to look into the director's personal history and consider why he might want to make such a statement about death and life.

- My all-time favorite film franchise, [_The Trip_](https://www.criterionchannel.com/the-trip), is still on the Criterion Channel, including the all of original television series that were later cut into the movies. I'm on my second watch in three months. I recommend you fit in a viewing before summer proper is over.

- By the way: newly obsessed with Ethel Cain.
