Evidently, [I watched 299 films last year](https://letterboxd.com/jenmyers/year/2025/). I almost wish I had been paying attention to that number so I could have rounded it out nicely at 300, but I suppose this is why I should always watch yet one more movie. Let that be a lesson to us all. In any case, here are the films I liked most in 2025.

### New Releases (Non-Horror Division)

- [_It Was Just an Accident_](https://letterboxd.com/film/it-was-just-an-accident/): I’ve never seen a Jafar Panahi film and not been overwhelmed by its quiet mastery, and every one he makes seems more and more masterful. This one is subtly breathtaking and astonishing in its implications. Easily my favorite of the year.
- [_Resurrection_](https://letterboxd.com/film/resurrection-2025/): I’m a devotee of Bi Gan’s 2018 film [_Long Day’s Journey into Night_](https://letterboxd.com/film/long-days-journey-into-night-2018/), and he took everything he started playing with in that film and ran with it straight to a whole different dimension. _Resurrection_ is mind-bending in the best way, a deconstruction and reconstruction of the idea of film in the course of a single film. Did I cry, for reasons I couldn’t begin to articulate? Perhaps. What’s a movie theater experience without a few tears, anyway.
- [_One Battle After Another_](https://letterboxd.com/film/one-battle-after-another/): I almost didn’t go see it. I like PTA’s work, but, from early reports and impressions, I couldn’t wrap my head around what he was doing with this one. But the film itself comes together. A thoroughly modern drama of people in extreme situations grounded with comedy and affection. It could so easily be trite, insincere, overwrought and/or offensive. It’s not.
- [_The Secret Agent_](https://letterboxd.com/film/the-secret-agent-2025/): A stylish Brazilian crime thriller with more humor than it has any right to pull off and a suavely knockout performance from Wagner Moura, who should cross over into all sorts of stardom after this.
- [_Wake Up Dead Man_](https://letterboxd.com/film/wake-up-dead-man/): What can I say—I’ve been a Rian Johnson fan from the beginning and I’m also a big fan of arch murder mysteries, so I enjoy the Benoit Blanc series. This one sacrifices some time with the colorful ensemble cast for the sake of more time with Josh O’Connor’s central character, but it’s a fair trade.
- [_Die My Love_](https://letterboxd.com/film/die-my-love/): Is there another working filmmaker as well acquainted with women’s madness as Lynne Ramsey? I’ve never encountered a film that describes the postpartum state of mind anywhere near as well as this film does, although I think that particular point is destined to go over most people’s heads. It’s dreamy and dissociative and, for anyone who’s been through similar situations, terrifyingly real.
- [_Videoheaven_](https://letterboxd.com/film/videoheaven/): If you want to watch a three-hour-long in-depth video essay about the history of the video rental store as represented in depictions of video stores in film and television, then you’re going to want to seek out _Videoheaven_. And congratulations for being the same type of weirdo as I am.

### New Releases (Horror Division)

- [_Sinners_](https://letterboxd.com/film/sinners-2025/): A capital-M movie with scope, depth and character, plus the courage to go full genre. Bloody and audacious, it nails everything it goes for and it’s a hell of a good time. I saw it twice in the theaters, the second time in 70mm, and I’m not sure if I want to experience it any other way. We’re so lucky to have movies.
- [_The Shrouds_](https://letterboxd.com/film/the-shrouds/): Characteristic of David Cronenberg’s later work in that you have to really, really think about it. My second watch clicked into place the futility of trying to puzzle it out, which I loved not only as a philosophical point but as a (probably) unintentional commentary on the current misguided mania for puzzling out art. There are no answers. There is just the experience.
- [_Cloud_](https://letterboxd.com/film/cloud-2024/): On one hand a deft, self-aware action thriller, and on the other a cold, brutal observation of late-stage capitalism and the double-edged sword of digital interconnectivity. Kiyoshi Kurosawa always gets to the dark, existential heart of it.
- [_Fréwaka_](https://letterboxd.com/film/frewaka/): Modern folk horror has begun, for me, to tip into cliché and occasional emptiness, so I appreciated the infusion of passion and perspective into the genre that _Fréwaka_ provides. Outstanding sophomore film from Aislinn Clarke, exactly the sort of horror film I love to see women making.
- [_Chain Reactions_](https://letterboxd.com/film/chain-reactions-2024/): Alexandre O. Philippe has staked out his own territory of genre film nerd documentaries and now that he seems to have settled into a format of consecutive film interview/essays, his work can concentrate on the input of his interviewees/essayists and is thus overall getting steadily stronger. _Chain Reactions_ gathers an inspired group of filmmakers, fans and critics to discuss _Texas Chain Saw Massacre_. It’s the right people in the right format with an endlessly fascinating subject, and it makes for a worthwhile doc.

Speed round:

[_Frankenstein_](https://letterboxd.com/film/frankenstein-2025/) (deeply uneven for me, but the parts that worked for me really worked for me), [_28 Years Later_](https://letterboxd.com/film/28-years-later/) (maybe a case of too-low expectations, but I ended up liking it far more than I thought I would), [_Influencers_](https://letterboxd.com/film/influencers-2025/) (slick, effective, star Cassandra Naud continues to fascinate and I hope they keep this series going), [_Dead Mail_](https://letterboxd.com/film/dead-mail/) (also uneven but with enough originality and panache to be worth mentioning), [_Night of the Reaper_](https://letterboxd.com/film/night-of-the-reaper-2025/) (my pick for surprise confection of the year: a fun, light slasher throwback), [_V/H/S/Halloween_](https://letterboxd.com/film/v-h-s-halloween/) (I’m always mixed on the V/H/S/ series but for some reason I liked this one a lot more than previous ones, and Alex Ross Perry’s entry is genuinely harrowing).
