### Links

- [The end of the roadside attraction.](https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/2025/04/09/the-end-of-roadside-attractions/) (Although as long as I'm alive, any and all roadside attractions will have at least one dedicated patron.)

- The older I get, the more I realize how messed up it was to be a teenage girl in the 1990s. The sexism in the media was insidious and I feel like only recently have we collectively realized it. Which is why I always like reading about the women I admired from that era talking about how they survived. Case in point: [Shirley Manson](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2025/apr/14/ive-pulled-myself-out-of-a-very-dark-abyss-garbages-shirley-manson-on-depression-sexism-dodgy-hips-and-happiness).

- Kayleigh Donaldson on how [influencer routines are just anti-feminism dressed up as self-care](https://www.pajiba.com/miscellaneous/down-with-the-morning-shed-influencer-routines-are-just-antifeminism-dressed-up-as-selfcare.php).

- Always up for a good article about the [long history of shifting tattoo perceptions and the conservative swing of anti-tattoo culture trends](https://www.dazeddigital.com/beauty/article/66383/1/are-the-tides-really-turning-against-tattoos).

- I don't know how many other people out there are into both metal and opera, but, if there are some, they can join me in enjoying this [Met Opera talk between the composer of the _Moby-Dick_ opera and Mastodon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AAYhUzojcNk).

- "That makes it very human. That’s always been my understanding of technology: It’s not a thing from outer space, it’s a projection of us. It can be hideously awful, murderous, and deadly, and it can also be fantastic and brilliant and life-affirming." [Jim Jarmusch interviews David Cronenberg.](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/david-cronenberg-by-jim-jarmusch)


### Reading/Watching/Listening

- I've spent most of the past three weeks at two different film festivals: the [Overlook Film Festival](https://www.overlookfilmfest.com/) in New Orleans and [David Lynch: Moving Through Time](https://musicboxtheatre.com/series-and-festivals/david-lynch-moving-through-time) back home in Chicago. As one might imagine, I saw quite a few movies. Many of them were excellent. All of them were good theater experiences. Notable among them are: [_Fréwaka_](https://letterboxd.com/film/frewaka/), [_Chain Reactions_](https://letterboxd.com/film/chain-reactions-2024/), [_The Shrouds_](https://letterboxd.com/film/the-shrouds/), [_Cloud_](https://letterboxd.com/film/cloud-2024/), [_I Know Catherine, The Log Lady_](https://letterboxd.com/film/i-know-catherine-the-log-lady/), [_Eraserhead_](https://letterboxd.com/film/eraserhead/) and [_Twin Peaks: Fire Walk with Me_](https://letterboxd.com/film/twin-peaks-fire-walk-with-me/).

- I read Rachel Monroe's [_Savage Appetites: Four True Stories of Women, Crime, and Obsession_](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/742075ef-691f-4916-bf53-00b5472d1667) and its only failing was that I wanted several more volumes of it.

- I'm currently reading the first in Eugene Thacker's "Horror of Philosophy" series, [_In the Dust of This Planet_](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/44526346-c2c1-46bb-b5d1-38dc68a00e5e), and it is fascinating. I feel these books are more well known in philosophy circles than in horror circles, but I think they would be of deep interest to those who like to think about the mechanics of horror.
