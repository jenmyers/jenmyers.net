### Links

- "There's something genuinely countercultural about rejecting massive platforms, tech surveillance, and optimized algorithms: you are choosing to operate outside the norms, outside the system that the web has become. You're not feeding the machine. You're building for your self. You're making something because you want to make it, on your own terms, and you're putting it where anyone can find it, if they want to." [The artisanal web.](https://another.rodeo/artisanal-web/)

- [What we think is a decline in literacy is a design problem.](https://aeon.co/essays/what-we-think-is-a-decline-in-literacy-is-a-design-problem)

- [Why consciousness is more likely a property of life than of computation and why creating conscious, or even conscious-seeming AI, is a bad idea.](https://www.noemamag.com/the-mythology-of-conscious-ai/)

- Let's lay the debate over the assumed misogyny of _Blue Velvet_ to final rest with [this essay from Willow Maclay about how Isabella Rossellini crafted the role of Dorothy Vallens](https://www.rogerebert.com/women-writers-week/the-mysteries-of-dorothy-vallens-on-isabella-rossellini-in-blue-velvet).

- "Self-deception need not involve false belief: just as the deceiver can attempt to produce a belief which is — as it happens — true, so too a self-deceiver can set herself to believe what is in fact true. A canny self-deceiver can focus on accurate but irrelevant observations as a way of denying a truth that is importantly relevant to her immediate projects." [On the positive power of self-deception.](https://www.themarginalian.org/2026/03/13/user-friendly-self-deception-amelie-rorty/)

### Reading/Watching/Listening

- I've just started it, but so far Will Sloan's new biography of Ed Wood, [_Ed Wood: Made in Hollywood USA_](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/e50c64e3-1d18-4a99-87c8-f1591e507c6e), is great.

- Winter is dragging its feet out the door, so I've been bolstering my spirits by reading my way through the [Miss Fisher mystery series](https://app.thestorygraph.com/series/1217). I'm a big fan of the television series so I worried that the books wouldn't make enough of their own impression—but that is very much not the case. They have enough style and substance in their own right.

- I saw [_The Bride!_](https://letterboxd.com/jenmyers/film/the-bride-2026/). It was messy but not without its delights.

- I dug up [_City that Never Sleeps_](https://letterboxd.com/film/city-that-never-sleeps/) mostly for its vintage Chicago content, but turns out it's a sharp, unique film noir that is worth seeking out.

- A couple of weeks ago, I went to see a second show on Nine Inch Nails' latest tour. It was a good time. I'm not going to post an awful phone video of that, though. Instead, here's a favorite of mine from 2006, featuring Peter Murphy (who, a couple of years after this, put on probably my favorite show I've ever seen in my life). Cheers.
